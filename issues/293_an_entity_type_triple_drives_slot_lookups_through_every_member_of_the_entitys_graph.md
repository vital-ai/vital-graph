## What it is

The standard KG shape "entities of type T, with slot S":

```sparql
?d   haley:hasKGEntityType <T> .
?s   haley:hasKGGraphURI   ?d ;
     haley:hasKGSlotType   <S> ;
     haley:hasTextSlotValue ?v .
```

is planned as: find the entities of type T, then for each one expand **every
object whose `hasKGGraphURI` is that entity** (its frames, slots, edges), then
keep the ones whose `hasKGSlotType` is S. On the production UW space each
decision entity's graph has ~1,088 members, so 1,159 decisions become 1,261,467
rows before the slot-type filter keeps 9,318 of them.

The selective side is the slot type: `(context, hasKGSlotType, <S>)` is one index
range of a few thousand rows, and each slot's `hasKGGraphURI` is then a PK lookup
by subject. The plan goes the other way because Postgres estimates the graph
fan-out at **9** per entity.

## Why the estimate is wrong, and why local missed it

The 9 is Postgres's own column estimate for `object_uuid = <value>` over the
whole quad table. It is not `rdf_stats`. Most object values are referenced a
handful of times, but `hasKGGraphURI`'s few objects are referenced ~1,000 times
each. A per-column statistic cannot express a per-predicate fan-out, and
extended statistics on (predicate, object) give a combined n_distinct, not the
fan-out of one predicate's objects.

The local UW space has 12 decisions with graphs of the same width, i.e. ~13k
probes, so every query ran in well under a second. Cost is ∝ entities ×
graph size, so production will keep getting worse as decisions accumulate.

## Secondary findings in the same plans

- **The correlated NOT EXISTS.** The only-cause query's `FILTER NOT EXISTS`
  is a per-row SubPlan (`Join Filter: NOT EXISTS(SubPlan 1)`) that builds its
  own hash for each outer row, on top of the fan-out. This is why it times
  out rather than taking 10 s. Check it separately once the fan-out is fixed.
- **Heap fetches** (228,586 on one index-only scan): the space's visibility map
  is far behind. This is consistent with `issues/136`, and small next to the
  fan-out.
- **The page sorts before LIMIT.** The decisions page sorts after joining all
  fields for all decisions. This is real but secondary: the fan-out is ~95 % of
  its time.

## General solution — plan and progress

**The root, stated once.** Postgres estimates a quad self-join from per-column
statistics. Two consequences recur through this class:

- it cannot see a predicate's PER-OBJECT (or per-subject) fan-out: a
  `hasKGGraphURI` probe by object is estimated at ~10 rows and returns ~1,000;
- it underestimates MID-FREQUENCY `(predicate, object)` pairs: one slot type's
  1,159 slots is estimated at 3, even with `quad_po` MCV statistics at target
  1000, because the top 1,000 pairs of a 14M-quad space are all larger.

Every earlier fix routed around one instance (`161`'s `entity_slot_sort`,
`118`'s UNION, `218`'s rewritten SPARQL, this issue's `slot_group_cte`).
`119` §9 records that feeding the estimate to Postgres is not the remedy. The
general fix is for the GENERATOR to own selectivity: price every leaf and
join from statistics it holds, choose the driving pattern by that price, and
emit a form that does not depend on Postgres agreeing.

**Stages.** Each is measured before the next starts, on the shapes of every
issue in the class, not only this one.

1. **Fan-out statistics.** Per predicate: distinct objects and distinct
   subjects, so mean subjects-per-object and objects-per-subject are exact
   (today the only source is `rdf_stats`' top pairs, biased high).
   `recompute_stats_tables` already streams every pair; add the counts there,
   to `rdf_pred_stats` (migration script, `migrate_*`), load them in
   `_load_quad_stats`. Price a variable-ended leaf whose other end is bound as
   its fan-out, not its whole predicate.
2. **A BGP cardinality model in the generator.** Join-by-join estimates from
   exact pair counts (`rdf_stats`, `extra_quad_stats`, FTS/range/IN counts) and
   stage 1's fan-outs. Tested by comparing its estimates, and Postgres', with
   `EXPLAIN ANALYZE` actuals on the fixtures. It must beat Postgres where
   Postgres is wrong, or nothing after it is justified.
3. **Choose the driver by the model** (`161`'s "anchor on the most selective
   constant") everywhere the generator picks one: `reorder_joins`' root,
   `semijoin._split_bgp`'s anchor (structural today), `selective-driven`.
4. **Enforce the order without Postgres' estimates.** The materialised star
   (`slot_group_cte`, generalised): any subject star whose model-predicted
   expansion exceeds its pinned size, for any predicate, not just
   `hasKGGraphURI`. Then the BGPs it declines today, those containing
   edge/`frame_slot` tables.
5. **Verification across workloads.** A perf-suite bench for the
   wide-graph shape (nothing there exercises it today), `lead_nurture_grouped`'s
   KGQuery text-search and frame paths, `uw_prodshape`, regression cases for
   161/118/045/138/218, and a replay of production's `slow_query` records.

### Stage 2 — the generator's cardinality model (flat quad BGPs, 2026-10-22)

`vitalgraph/db/sparql_sql/bgp_cardinality.py`, not yet wired into any
decision. Per join step: a scanned leaf is its exact pair count (or the sum of
its `IN` list's pairs); a probe by object is `row_count / object_count`
(stage 1); a probe by subject is one value; a RESTRICTED leaf (constant
object, IN, range, FTS) probed by subject is CONTAINMENT, rows ×
min(1, restricted rows / distinct values of the joined variable), with
distinct values tracked per variable; both ends bound is `1 / object_count`.

**Measured** by `test_scripts_internal/kg_uw_decision_reasons/model_vs_pg.py`:
for each flat BGP, along the join order the generator emits, every prefix's
model estimate, Postgres' `EXPLAIN` estimate and actual `count(*)`; q-error =
max(est/actual, actual/est).

| corpus | prefixes | geo-mean q-error, model | Postgres | model more accurate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| UW dashboard, `uw_prodshape` (tuned on) | 121 | **1.42** (max 19.7) | 189.85 (max 1,033,972) | 117 |
| lead queries, `sp_lead_synth_100k` (held out) | 33 | **3.73** | 40.97 | 29 |

Postgres' estimate of the plain constant pair `(hasKGEntityType, Decision)`
is 1 for 1,159 rows; of the `hasKGGraphURI` expansion, 1 for 1,033,972 on UW
and 128,619 for 10,654,000 on leads. The model: 586,271 and 11,445,622.

How it got there, each step measured: independence on a constant-object probe
gave 2.58 / max 847 on UW (frame type and slot type are correlated: NAICS is
10% of the slots in its frame type, 0.1% of all slots); containment, 1.42 /
max 19.7. Capping ROWS rather than distinct values was wrong in reversed
orders (unit test). An `IN` list (a pushed VALUES) priced as its whole
predicate gave 6.1 on the lead filter; summing its pairs, 1.0.

**Known limits:**

- *Mean fan-out cannot see per-object skew or zero.* On leads, child frames are
  never the object of `hasFrameGraphURI` (slots point at the root frame), so a
  frame-type pin probed by object returns 0. The model said 6.8M (Postgres
  123,701). Overestimates are the safe direction for a guard, wrong for a
  driver choice.
- *Containment vs independence.* Containment is exact for correlated pins (UW)
  and 6× high on uniform data (leads, a second slot type pinned on probed
  members: 100,000 vs 16,486; independence over the variable's domain would
  give ~17.7k). Choosing per step needs co-occurrence statistics
  (characteristic sets); not built.
- *Not modelled:* edge and `frame_slot` tables, so KGQuery's frame paths
  (both modes compile to `vg-direct:` edges) are not covered; EXISTS
  sub-patterns, whose constants are resolved after emit.
- Both corpora are small (9 + 6 queries). Postgres' errors are orders of
  magnitude; the model's are mostly under 2x, with the exceptions above.

### Stage 3 — the model chooses the driver (2026-10-22)

**Measured first: does a better order change what runs?** Four variants of
this generator on the dashboard corpus, rows identical in every case:

| query | A: HEAD | B: model order written | C: B + `join_collapse_limit = 1` | D: `slot_group_cte` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| by_reason | 1.03M loops, 21.8 s | unchanged | **6,950, 1.1 s** | 6,950, 1.1 s |
| latest_per_deal | 1.03M, 49.6 s | unchanged | **3,991, 1.3 s** | 3,991, 0.7 s |
| only_cause | 7.0M, 90 s | unchanged | **1.9M, 22 s** | 7.0M, 70 s |
| decision_page | 1.03M, 29.7 s | unchanged | 1.03M, 4.3 s | **3,991, 0.9 s** |
| category_page | 74,305, 12.7 s | unchanged | 9.2 s | **6,950, 3.1 s** |
| naics_for_category | 2.4 s | unchanged | 2.4 s | **1.1 s** |

- B: writing the order changes nothing. With eight or fewer relations
  Postgres re-plans it from its own estimates.
- C fixes the ANCHORED cases (the entity type in the same BGP) and is the first
  thing to help only_cause (its NOT EXISTS body is ordered too). It cannot fix
  a BGP whose ?d arrives from OUTSIDE (OPTIONAL fields, VALUES-split joins):
  Postgres still probes it per decision.
- D fixes exactly those, by materialising the BGP.
- C + D together, enforced everywhere, made category_page 3.1 s -> 9.1 s: the
  enforced order inside its reason BGP was the MODEL'S MISTAKE (the mean
  fan-out of `hasFrameGraphURI`, 11.6, against 229 for reason frames, stage 2's
  known limit), and enforcement made it binding.

**The rule.** Enforce the model's order only when it predicts at least
**10x** over the default (`bgp_cardinality.ENFORCE_MIN_GAIN`): above the
model's measured error band (1.4-3.7x geo-mean, ~6x worst non-zero). Below
it, the default order stands and `slot_group_cte` handles the unanchored
case. A BGP with a text, range or FTS anchor keeps the default always
(`reorder_joins` roots on those for measured reasons, 181/202). The threshold
was chosen from stage 2's error, but with this corpus in view: the held-out
lead queries and the perf suite are what test it.

Code: `bgp_cardinality.choose_order` / `enforced_order`; `emit_bgp` takes the
model's order before (and instead of) `slot_group_cte`; constraint placement
factored out of `reorder_joins` as `assign_constraints`; the
`fixed_join_order` flag (emit context -> `GenerateResult` -> the space impl's
`SET LOCAL join_collapse_limit = 1`, beside `needs_ordered_scan`'s
`enable_sort`). Decisions recorded as `model_join_order`.

## Attempted 2026-10-22, and reverted: drop the `IS NULL OR` join guards across a VALUES split

**Hypothesis:** `category_page`'s remaining 3 s was its `IS NULL OR` join
conditions. `emit_join._always_bound` declines to drop a compatible-mapping
guard whenever ANY VALUES table sits in the subtree (`_has_values_table`),
because `compute_scope` does not model UNDEF. But UNDEF can only unbind a
variable the VALUES block binds. In `category_page`, `VALUES ?code {…}` sat

widely, its search must include slot groups as units. Each group is one leaf

shared variable. The immediate rule is that a model order never displaces slot
groups: groups first, then the model orders what remains. Enforcement
mechanics are not the bottleneck; the statement-wide pin worked in P.

## Attempted 2026-10-22, and reverted: lift `only_cause`'s correlated FILTER out of the NOT EXISTS body

6 h (`EDGE_FANOUT_INTERVAL_S`) and by `resync_all`; loaded on every query
(`generator.py`, stage 2a.4).

**Its one consumer.** `emit_slice._emit_two_phase` calls
`sync_edge_fanout.assess_traversal` and declines the two-phase PAGING rewrite
when the probe's traversal amplifies forward. Nothing else in the query path
reads it (`choose_direction` reads pair counts, not this table; the docstring
in `sync_entity_fanout` that calls it the "traversal-direction gate" input
overstates its role).

**Cost.** Rebuild 0.9 s at 0.5M edges, 1.5 s at 1.0M, 23 s at 5.3M (vg test
stack, linear in edges), every 6 h per space; plus a load on every query.

**Evidence, all 2026-10-22:**

- Perf suite on fixtures WITH the table computed (2-14 buckets each, up to
  5.3M edges): 739 queries loaded it, all non-empty; the guard ran up to 101
  times and declined 0 times. The only UNSAFE verdicts came from
  `tests/performance/test_relation_traversal.py::test_the_inverted_relation_is_not_walked_backward`
  calling `assess_traversal` directly, not from a query.
- The same suite with the table loaded EMPTY: 0 plan-shape changes in 167
  benches; 1 status change, `query.relation.direction_choice`, the test of the
  statistic's own values; total work moved > 10% on 2 benches (+12% buffers,
  `traversal.skew2k` depth 2) although both runs made identical decisions, so
  run-to-run drift. Both runs on the same, slightly stale stats; not yet
  repeated under the measurement protocol (fresh stats).
- Production's slow-query log, 7 days to 2026-10-22: 222 records, ONE
  edge-pattern KGQuery, and it is a count, which never reaches the paging
  guard. No query in the log could have reached the consumer.
- Discarded as evidence: an integration-suite count (its spaces never compute
  the table: 1,061 of 1,063 loads were empty, so it could not show a positive).

**Caveats.** Queries under 1 s are not in production's slow log, and a guard
that works would keep its queries fast, so the log cannot prove a negative on
its own. The perf fixtures are mostly synthetic.

**Options.**

1. Stop the 6-hourly rebuild and the per-query load; keep the table and
   `compute_edge_fanout` as an on-demand operator diagnostic, as was done for
   `entity_fanout` (2026-09-13). The guard then sees an empty table and never
   declines, which is what it already does in every measured workload.
2. Remove it entirely: table, rebuild, loader, the guard's fan-out branch.
3. Keep it, if a workload is found where the guard declines and that decline
   is the faster plan, measured.

Whichever is chosen also decides its own tests: `tests/integration/test_edge_fanout.py`
and `test_edge_fanout_tolerates_untyped_edges.py`,
`tests/unit/test_edge_fanout_refresh.py`, and the perf benches
`query.relation.direction_choice` / `test_relation_traversal.py`, which test
the statistic's values rather than a query.

Evidence files (gitignored): `test_scripts_internal/kg_uw_decision_reasons/perf/fanout_perf_{count,off}.*`,

## This is a recurring class, not a new defect

The pattern has come up at least eight times. An unselective structural anchor
(almost always `?entity hasKGEntityType <T>`, i.e. the whole population) drives
a per-row expansion through a quad self-join. Postgres's per-column,
independence-based estimates (`rows=1–12`) make the expansion look cheap. The
selective constant (a slot type or value) is applied only after the fan-out.
What 293 adds is the expansion running through a grouping-URI predicate
(`hasKGGraphURI`) whose object is a variable.

| issue | the same shape | what was done |
|---|---|---|
| `161` | **Root cause 2026-09-05: "the anchor is chosen structurally, not by selectivity."** `semijoin._split_bgp` anchors on the quads that bind only the projected variable, which for an entity query is always the type triple. "Selectivity never enters the choice." | The proposed general fix, **"anchor on the most selective CONSTANT and confirm upward"** (priced from `rdf_stats` / `absence_bounds`), was never built. The shape was served instead by `fast_slot_filter` on `entity_slot_sort`, for KGQuery only. `MAX_PROBE_CANDIDATES` is a partial guard. |
| `118` | Adding `hasKGEntityType <Lead>` merged everything into one flat BGP, so `selective-driven` declined ("no 2-child JOIN within 6 hops"). | Downgraded to fixture-specific; fix declined. Same mechanism as here: the type triple folds into the flat join tree. |
| `045` (archived) | A specific entity type folded into the BGP, so the semi-join never ran. | `_split_bgp` / `_split_anchors`: a general fix, but only around the projected variable. |
| `138` | The semi-join anchored on `hasKGEntityType NurtureAction` and probed once per entity (76,254 loops). | The cause was a bug (truncated term identity), not an estimate. |
| `048` | Adding a slot-type constraint made the `frame_entity` collapse decline (~50x buffers). | Absorbed as a correlated EXISTS. **Problem 4:** an uncorrelated IN gave identical buffers (Postgres normalised it back), and a MATERIALIZED fence was 149x slower. |
| `183` | `frame_entity` hardcoded two slot types. | Replaced by `frame_slot` + `frame_type_absorbable`. Lesson: constants resolved in a CTE are invisible at plan time. |
| `218` | `?slot hasKGGraphURI ?entityUri . ?entity URIProp ?entityUri` walked all 84,291 entities. | Fixed in the generated SPARQL by binding the entity straight from `hasKGGraphURI` (20–28 s to 0.59 s). |
| `119` §9 | — | **"The remedy is do not fix the estimate."** Feeding Postgres the exact count flipped a 421 ms plan to 8.5–109 s elsewhere. |

Also: `high_cardinality_slot_value_query_plan.md` (predicate/object
independence, 91 vs 34,644; `CREATE STATISTICS quad_po` shipped as "necessary,
not sufficient"; semi-join cannot project values (`semijoin.py:736`) or cross a
GROUP BY (`:753`)). `work_proportional_to_answer_plan.md` (loops ≫ rows is the
signal; "force the order, or inject the cardinality"). `issues/192` / `208`:
`hasKGGraphURI` fan-out exists in only one fixture (`lead_nurture_grouped`, ~745
per entity), which is why fixtures keep missing this class.

## Why nothing that exists handles it

- **`frame_slot` collapse.** It matches an arm (edge + `hasKGSlotType` + slot
  value sharing a slot variable) joined to the frame. Here the slot is tied to
  ?d by `hasKGGraphURI`, not an edge chain. `{space}_frame_slot` has no column
  for the slot's owning entity or graph (`frame_uuid, slot_uuid, role_uuid,
  entity_uuid, context_uuid, frame_type_uuid`; `entity_uuid` is the slot's
  entity VALUE). So it cannot answer this shape, even though
  `ensure_frame_slot_table` fires.
- **Semi-join split** (045/161): anchors on the type triple by construction,
  cannot project slot values, and refuses GROUP BY. The dashboard queries need
  all three.
- **`selective-driven`**: needs a two-child JOIN; one flat BGP gives it none (118).
- **`reorder_joins` / pair stats** (`rdf_stats`, `edge_fanout`): price constant
  (predicate, object) pairs or edge types. A `hasKGGraphURI` leaf with a variable
  object has no row in either.
- **`entity_slot_sort` / `fast_slot_filter`**: keyed by (context, entity type,
  frame-type path, slot type, value, entity). That is exactly "slot S of an
  entity of type T", and `208` projects slot values from it in 3–6 ms. But
  only `POST /kgqueries` reads it; hand-written SPARQL never reaches it.

## Fix, 2026-10-22: the slot group as a MATERIALIZED CTE

`vitalgraph/db/sparql_sql/slot_group_cte.py`, called from
`emit_bgp._emit_flat_inner`. The **slot group** is the `hasKGGraphURI` leaf
(variable object) plus every other leaf on the same slot subject with a constant
predicate: slot type, value, frame link. It is emitted as one `MATERIALIZED`
CTE, driven from the group's cheapest member (the pin). Inside, that is the pin
plus primary-key lookups by subject, at the pin's cost whatever the estimates.
Outside, the CTE is a set computed once, and references to the moved aliases
are renamed to its columns.

Why this works where the three restrictions below did not: those left index
lookups inside whatever Postgres re-ran per outer row. A materialised set has
nothing to re-run. Postgres underestimates only SMALL pairs (a large pair is in
the MCV list and gets hashed), and rescanning a small materialised set is cheap.

Two refinements, each found by a regression in the A/B:

- **Unanchored BGP → materialise the whole BGP**, term joins included. When ?d
  is bound outside the BGP (an OPTIONAL field; a VALUES-split join whose
  `IS NULL OR` compatibility condition cannot be hashed), Postgres nested-loops
  and re-runs the whole BGP per outer row. by_outcome without this: 1.1M term
  lookups. naics_for_category: 3.8M loops.
- **Only a constant-object leaf anchors ?d.** A second group's graph link also
  binds ?d, but anchors nothing. Counting it hid the unanchored case.

Gate: fire when `pinned × 2 < min(anchor × mean graph size, all hasKGGraphURI rows)`.
The pin is the cheapest member by any count the IR carries (pair, FTS match,
range), so KGQuery's text search is sized by its match count. Decisions are
recorded as `slot_group_cte` in `plan_decisions`.

## Attempted 2026-10-21, and reverted: restricting the `hasKGGraphURI` leaf

Three forms were measured on `uw_prodshape` against the original SQL, on seven
dashboard queries. Measured by busiest-node loops and full result multisets,
since the machine was swapping and wall times are not trustworthy. Rows were
identical in every comparison that finished.

| form | by_reason | regressions |
|---|---|---|
| **fence**: the leaf as `(SELECT * ... WHERE subject IN (pin) OFFSET 0)`, gated on pinned < anchor × mean graph size | 1,033,972 → 6,950 | by_outcome 1,159 → 1.1M; naics 3,292 → 3.4M; all_of, category_page and decision_page time out (> 120 s) |
| **block the by-object probe**: `COALESCE(q.object_uuid, q.object_uuid)` on every such leaf | 1,033,972 → 6,950 | by_outcome → 1.1M; latest_per_deal → 4.6M; decision_page times out |
| block only leaves joined to an entity-type pattern in the same BGP | 1,033,972 → 6,950 | latest_per_deal 1.03M → 4.6M; decision_page times out (was 27 s) |

The `MATERIALIZED` CTE form of the fence was also tried on by_outcome: no
better, because the whole inner side of the left join re-runs per outer row,
not just the fenced set.

**Why every form fails the same way: a SECOND misestimate, in the other
direction.** Postgres prices a mid-frequency `(predicate, object)` pair, such as
one slot type's slots, at a few rows:
`(hasKGSlotType, UwNbdOutcome)` is 3 estimated against 1,159 actual, and
`(hasKGSlotType, SFRefOpportunityId)` a handful against 3,991. That holds with
`stat_<space>_quad_po (mcv, ndistinct)` at target 1000: in a 14M-quad space the
top 1,000 pairs are all larger, so these never enter the MCV list. Whatever the
rewrite restricts, Postgres then puts the "tiny" pinned side inside a nested
loop and re-runs it per outer row (1,159 × 965, 2.7M, 4.6M rows through join
filters). The original plans were bad because of the fan-out underestimate.
The rewritten ones are bad because of the pair underestimate. Any rewrite that
only changes which access paths Postgres can use is at the mercy of both.

So the earlier lesson holds (`119` §9, `048` Problem 4,
`work_proportional_to_answer_plan.md` §8): changing what the planner SEES does
not change what it BELIEVES. A fix has to either control the order itself or
avoid the quad self-join. The code and harnesses are kept, gitignored, in
`test_scripts_internal/kg_uw_decision_reasons/attempts/` and `ab_fence.py`.

**Also seen, separate:** in `latest_per_deal_by_outcome`, the uncorrelated
`{ SELECT ?opp (MAX(?at)) ... GROUP BY ?opp }` is re-executed once per outer
row (`GroupAggregate ... loops=1159`). It is uncorrelated, so it should run once.
